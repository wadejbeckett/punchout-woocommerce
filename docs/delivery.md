# Delivery

## Current behaviour (0.5.0)

A visit shops the ordinary WooCommerce cart and leaves through the shop's **classic checkout**, where **PunchOut** is the only payment method offered. Its Submit (the method's place-order label) places the order, builds the cXML `PunchOutOrderMessage` from it and posts the message to the visit's BrowserFormPost from the handoff page. The order stays on the store as a non-payable **Punchout Quote**. There is no payment step and no review page of the plugin's own: WooCommerce's checkout is the review.

**The address.** The buyer chooses the delivery address at checkout: one of the bound account's saved shipping addresses (through the site's address-book plugin, when it has one; the shipping section opens on the account's default when the store's "Shipping destination" is the customer's shipping address), or a one-off address typed there, which stays on that order only. Billing opens on the account's saved billing profile. **Checkout saves nothing to the bound account** during a visit (`Checkout\VisitAccount`): not its billing or shipping details, and not an address-book plugin's entries. Buyers cannot add or change saved addresses; shop administrators keep them on the account.

**The account's saved addresses (0.4.15, 0.5.0).** WooCommerce keeps one shipping address per customer. A site that wants several per account installs an address-book extension; the plugin finds it by shape, never by name: a PHP function called `get_address_book` (in any namespace) whose first parameter is typed `WC_Customer`, second the type, returning an object with `addresses()`, `default_key()` and `add()` (`Addresses\AccountBook`). The company delivery book of earlier releases stays an administrator surface (**Company delivery addresses** on the customer administration screen) and the source of `wp punchout migrate-addresses <connection> [--dry-run]`, which copies its enabled entries into the account's saved addresses once; no visit reads the book since 0.5.0, and the connection setting that let buyers add to it is retired (the column stays and reads off). The plugin's own code map per connection on the account (`_pow_account_codes_{connection id}`, `Addresses\AccountCodes`) is kept for the Quotes that carry a code; the order's cXML sends none.

**Delivery cost is WooCommerce's.** The order keeps its normal shipping line, the store's own estimate. The plugin adds no delivery or tax wording anywhere a buyer reads; hiding rate amounts or totals from a buyer is theme or WooCommerce work. Only while the connection's **Send delivery cost** (`emit_delivery_line`) is on and the order's shipping total before tax is more than zero does the message add one quantity-one freight line, that total, named after the shipping method with the estimate sentence ("The delivery charge is an estimate; the supplier confirms the final amount before dispatch.", fixed and translatable, filter `punchout_delivery_estimate_note`; blank sends none), which then also leads DeliveryInstructions. Off, or on a free-shipping order (no 0.00 line, no sentence), the Total is the merchandise.

**ShipTo and DeliveryInstructions.** With **Send ShipTo** (`emit_ship_to`) on, the order's shipping address is sent as the full postal `ShipTo`. DeliveryInstructions carries the order note typed at checkout (cleaned of characters cXML cannot carry, capped at 2,000 characters), then each of the connection's **Order details sent** with a value, as "Label: value", joined by " | ": an order meta key, a label and a format, `text` or `unix_date` (a Unix timestamp sent as its UTC calendar day). A date plugin's timestamp and a checkout attachment plugin's plain file-name key reach the purchasing system this way; the file itself stays on the order. Every value listed travels to the purchasing system, and a key beginning with `_pow_` (the plugin's own) is refused. With Send ShipTo on, a delivery address with a street that cXML cannot carry is refused at checkout before any order exists, so an address never vanishes silently from the message. It travels on every merchandise `ItemDetail` while `delivery_notes_policy` is `item_detail_extrinsic`. See `README.md`, "Independent delivery configuration".

**Refusals.** Before any order exists checkout refuses a line without a SKU, a cart priced in another currency than the store's and a note cXML cannot carry, with a plain message naming the item. Another payment method forced into the form, the Store API checkout, order-pay and order-received are refused in a visit; PunchOut is refused outside one.

**Cancel and return.** `[punchout_abandon_button]` posts the empty message (the cXML cancel semantic) to `/punchout/return` and closes that one visit; with "Ask before leaving" on and a line in the cart it asks first (`question` attribute, two label settings). See `README.md`, "Leaving without a cart".

## What the old review stored (0.3.0 to 0.4.23), read-only

The plugin's own review page (`/punchout/confirm`, `[punchout_delivery_confirmation]`) retired in 0.5.0 with its consent snapshot, chooser, estimate note, attachment field, buyer-added addresses, preferred date (already gone in 0.4.23) and tax sentence. What it wrote is still read as stored, never written:

- **Visit rows** keep `delivery_choice` and `delivery_confirmation` (`Addresses\DeliveryData` decodes them: choice schema 1 with `provider` `native`, `company` or `account`, `source` `company_book`, `account_book`, `ship_to` or `customer`; confirmation schema 1 (9 keys) or 2 (10 keys, `preferred_delivery_date`)). Each schema's field set is exact; malformed JSON refuses rather than repairs. The cart commit clears both columns, so a current visit's are empty.
- **Punchout Quotes** returned before 0.5.0 carry `_pow_delivery_choice`, `_pow_delivery_confirmation`, `_pow_delivery_notes`, `_pow_delivery`, `_pow_delivery_code` and, before 0.4.23, `_pow_preferred_delivery_date`, read by `Orders\QuoteCompatibility` (an exact compatibility copy of the returned cart) and shown on the order screen and in the store e-mail. Their attachment (`_pow_attachment`) stays downloadable by staff from the order screen.
- **Collection** was derived from the stored `method_id` (`local_pickup`, `legacy_local_pickup`, `pickup_location`), a fixed list, for the note those Quotes carry.
